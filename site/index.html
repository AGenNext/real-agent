Agents you can put in production — and be accountable for.

A prompt

A workflow

An advisor

Recommends but cannot act, or acts with no governance. Not production-ready.

One real-world model

Twelve primitives. One coherent agent.

Agency is a graph, not a single object. Real Agent defines the canonical primitives and how they relate — implementation-neutral, machine-readable.

Identity

Who is acting.

Authority

What it is allowed to do — distinct from capability.

Objective

Why it is acting.

Context

What it knows now, traceable to source.

Decision

What it chose — the atomic, auditable unit.

Action

What it did, under authorization.

Outcome

What happened.

Memory

Semantic, episodic, procedural, working.

Policy

The rules that constrain it.

Trust

Measured confidence, continuously re-evaluated.

Evaluation

Evidence behind the trust.

Governance

Who is accountable.

The agent loop

Every pass produces a decision record.

  1. Observe
  2. Decide
  3. Authorize
  4. Act
  5. Evaluate
  6. Remember

Decisions cause actions, actions produce outcomes, outcomes update trust, and governance stays accountable for the whole chain — all on an append-only audit log.

Multi-team

One platform, many teams — each governing its own agents.

Teams operate as isolated tenants. A single identity can carry across teams, while authority, policy, data, and audit stay scoped to each. Onboard a department without entangling it with the rest of the organization.

  • Tenant isolation by namespace
  • Per-team authority & policy boundaries
  • Portable identity across teams
  • Independent audit and trust per team

Enterprise controls

Policy overrides intelligence.

Governance

Trust
